A trip to the Banggai Islands does not have to focus on just one well-known stop. Peleng Island has several natural attractions in its southern districts, particularly around Bulagi Selatan and Buko Selatan. Travelers planning a wider **Banggai Islands trip** can consider this area as part of a multi-day itinerary.

The important part is to avoid treating every place name as an easy same-day stop. Road conditions, ferry connections, local transport and visitor access need to be checked before setting a fixed schedule.

## Why explore southern Peleng?

A January 2026 report on a local tourism discussion described Bulagi Selatan and Buko Selatan as an interconnected area with several potential attractions. The places mentioned included Goa Babanang, Goa Susundeng, Teluk Luwok Balalon, Batualambun Waterfall, highland lakes and Sapelang Beach. This is a list of regional tourism potential, not confirmation that every site has visitor facilities or reliable access.

Choose one or two priority stops first. Then ask local residents or a guide which locations can realistically be visited during your dates.

## Goa Babanang: verify access first

Goa Babanang is listed as a cave attraction in the Babang area of Bulagi Selatan, Banggai Islands Regency. However, the Ministry of Tourism's Sisparnas attraction record currently marks the information as **not yet verified by the local Tourism Office**. Details such as visitor facilities, opening hours and ticket prices are not fully available.

Do not assume that visitor services are in place or that the cave is always accessible. Before adding it to your itinerary, confirm the exact location, route conditions, any required permission or local assistance, and the current situation with residents. Avoid entering cave sections whose safety is unknown.

## Lake Lemelu and other possible stops

Lake Lemelu is located in Lemelu Village, Bulagi Selatan District. Indonesia.travel describes it as a lake in the hills of Peleng Island, with water levels that change periodically. Its guide outlines an approach from Luwuk involving a motorboat to Sabang Village followed by a land transfer; it also mentions an alternative route through the Banggai Islands administrative centre.

Use this as an initial planning reference, not as a current transport timetable. Confirm the departure port, boat availability, onward vehicle and actual travel time before booking. If you are also interested in Goa Susundeng, Sapelang Beach or other nearby-area attractions, check whether their access routes can sensibly be combined.

## How to plan a southern Peleng itinerary

1. **Set your arrival point.** Decide whether you are starting in Luwuk or elsewhere in the Banggai Islands, then check the available boat connections.
2. **Choose priority destinations.** Build the trip around the places you most want to see instead of trying to collect every stop.
3. **Group stops by real access.** Ask a local guide whether two locations can fit into one day, based on current roads and travel times.
4. **Leave a buffer.** Avoid tight connections between boats, road transfers and flights.
5. **Check on-site requirements.** Ask about facilities, local fees, guides, trail conditions and visitor rules before departure.

## Plan your Banggai Islands trip with local support

Exploring southern Peleng requires more local coordination than simply selecting locations on a map. Share your dates, group size, arrival point, trip length and priority destinations so the route can be planned around actual conditions.
