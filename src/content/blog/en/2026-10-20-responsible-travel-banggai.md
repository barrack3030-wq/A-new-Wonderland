# Responsible Travel in Banggai: Protecting the Sea and Local Communities

The Banggai Islands are known for clear water, small islands, beaches, and distinctive marine life. These places are not only there to be enjoyed; they also need to be cared for. A few simple choices during a trip can reduce pressure on the environment and help tourism bring more benefits to local communities.

## Snorkel and explore the sea carefully

**Never touch or stand on coral.** Reefs can be damaged by hands, feet, or fins. Keep a respectful distance from coral and marine animals, do not chase wildlife for photographs, and follow your guide's advice about currents, depth, and safe areas.

Wear a life jacket when appropriate and do not enter the water when waves or currents are unsafe. Do not collect coral, shells containing living animals, fish, or other marine organisms as souvenirs. Observing wildlife from a distance is better for the animals and often makes for a more natural experience.

## Reduce waste on island trips

Bring a reusable water bottle, avoid disposable packaging where practical, and keep rubbish with you until you find a suitable disposal point. Do not leave cigarette butts, food wrappers, or other waste on beaches or boats. If there are no bins, ask your guide how to carry waste back to a place with proper facilities.

Never throw food scraps or other objects into the sea. Even small pieces of rubbish can harm marine life and pollute the shoreline.

## Respect village life and local residents

Coastal villages are communities, not just photo backdrops. Ask permission before photographing people, homes, or private activities. Dress appropriately for the setting, follow your host's guidance, and never enter private spaces without permission.

When buying food, hiring a boat or guide, or purchasing local crafts, agree on the price and terms beforehand. Whenever possible, use local services directly so that more of the tourism income stays in the communities you visit.

## Choose operators who prioritize safety and the environment

Before booking a boat trip or snorkeling tour, ask whether the operator provides life jackets, checks the weather, keeps passenger numbers within capacity, and explains environmental guidelines. Do not pressure boat crews to approach wildlife too closely or continue when conditions are unsafe.

Boat schedules, weather, and access to destinations can change. Confirm current conditions with a local operator or guide and be ready to adjust your plans.

## Learn about Banggai's natural heritage

The Banggai cardinalfish (*Pterapogon kauderni*) is known as a fish native to the Banggai Islands region. Distinctive wildlife like this is a reminder that the sea has ecological value beyond its scenic appeal. Observe marine animals without catching, feeding, or disturbing them or their habitat.

Do not assume every destination has the same access rules. Follow the directions of local managers, guides, and residents, especially where areas are restricted or undergoing restoration.

## A simple checklist for visitors

- Carry a refillable bottle and a bag for your rubbish.
- Do not touch coral or collect marine life.
- Wear a life jacket and listen to the safety briefing.
- Ask permission before photographing residents or private spaces.
- Confirm prices and service terms before paying.
- Support local businesses and respect village rules.
- Change or cancel sea activities if the weather or water conditions are unsafe.

## Explore Banggai with care

A good trip is not measured only by how many places you visit, but also by how you treat nature and the people who live there. By protecting the sea, reducing waste, respecting local culture, and choosing responsible services, visitors can help keep Banggai special for future travellers and local generations.

Operating details, destination access, and sea conditions can change. Check the latest guidance from local guides and destination managers before taking part in activities.
