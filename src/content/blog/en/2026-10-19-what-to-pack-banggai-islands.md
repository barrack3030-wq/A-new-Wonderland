A trip around Luwuk, Peleng Island and the Banggai Islands can include road journeys, boat transfers, swimming stops and nights in places where shops and services are limited. Packing well helps you stay comfortable without carrying more than you need.

Use this checklist as a starting point, then adjust it to your route, the season and the accommodation you have booked.

## The essentials at a glance

| Item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Small waterproof dry bag | Keeps your phone, documents and spare clothes dry on boats |
| Cash in Indonesian rupiah | ATMs and card payments may be unavailable in smaller communities |
| Sun protection | Tropical sun can be intense during boat trips and beach visits |
| Swimwear and quick-dry clothes | Useful for snorkeling, swimming and humid weather |
| Reusable water bottle | Helps reduce single-use plastic; refill where safe drinking water is available |
| Personal medication | Pharmacies and specific medicines may be difficult to find on smaller islands |
| Offline maps and saved bookings | Mobile signal can be inconsistent away from towns |

## Clothing and footwear

Bring lightweight, breathable clothing, plus one modest outfit for villages or visits to local communities. A light long-sleeved shirt can help with sun protection. Pack swimwear, quick-dry shorts and a change of clothes in a separate dry bag.

For footwear, use sandals or shoes that are comfortable when wet, and bring sturdy walking shoes if your itinerary includes waterfalls, uneven paths or caves. Avoid assuming every viewpoint or beach has a paved approach.

## For snorkeling and boat days

Bring a well-fitting mask if you prefer using your own, a rash guard or swim shirt, and a towel that dries quickly. Operators may provide equipment, but confirm this before departure. A waterproof phone pouch is useful, though it should not replace a proper dry bag for important items.

Follow your guide’s instructions, check sea conditions, and do not touch or stand on coral. If currents are strong or visibility is poor, choose a safer activity instead.

## Money, documents and connectivity

Carry enough rupiah for meals, small purchases, local transport and unexpected changes. Keep some cash separate from your main wallet. Save accommodation details, ferry or boat arrangements, and important contact numbers offline.

Bring identification and any documents required for your bookings. Download maps before leaving Luwuk, and tell someone your planned route if you will be travelling through areas with limited signal. A power bank is useful on long transfer days.

## Health and weather basics

Pack sunscreen, insect repellent, a small first-aid kit, any prescribed medication and basic personal-care supplies. Take a light rain jacket or compact poncho because tropical weather can change quickly. Bring a dry change of clothes after swimming or a wet boat crossing.

Do not rely on finding a particular medicine or specialist item on a small island. If you have a medical need, prepare supplies before leaving a larger town and seek professional advice about your own requirements.

## What not to overpack

Large suitcases can be awkward on small boats and narrow village paths. A backpack or soft-sided bag is often easier to handle. Leave valuables and unnecessary electronics at your accommodation when possible, and avoid bringing disposable items you can replace with reusable alternatives.

## Before leaving Luwuk

- Confirm boat and ferry times directly with the operator; schedules can change.
- Check whether your accommodation provides towels, drinking water and snorkeling equipment.
- Withdraw cash and buy essential supplies before travelling to smaller islands.
- Check the marine forecast and ask local operators about conditions.
- Keep a buffer between separate transport connections.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need cash in the Banggai Islands?
It is wise to carry cash. Payment options vary by location, and card or ATM access may be limited outside larger towns.

### Should I bring my own snorkeling gear?
It depends on your preference and the operator. Ask what is included, and bring your own mask if fit and comfort are important to you.

### Is a suitcase suitable?
A compact suitcase can work, but a backpack or soft bag is usually easier for boat transfers and uneven paths.

A little preparation makes island travel smoother. Pack light, protect your essentials from water, and leave room in your plan for changing sea and transport conditions.
