You have done Lombok. Maybe you have surfed the breaks around Kuta, hiked a stretch of Rinjani, or simply let the days blur together on the Gilis. Now you are standing at a crossroads that most travellers never reach: the edge of the usual Indonesian route. The question of where to go after Lombok is really a question about how far you are willing to go.

For Australian travellers in particular, the answer increasingly points east, toward Central Sulawesi and the Banggai Islands. This is not the Bali of ten years ago. It is a working archipelago where ferries run on island time, where the bluest lake you have ever seen sits at the end of an unmaintained road, and where you are more likely to share a reef with a local fisherman than another foreign tourist. This guide covers the practical routes, the realistic travel times, and the trade-offs so you can decide whether Banggai is your next step, or whether another destination in eastern Indonesia fits better.

## Why Central Sulawesi and Banggai?

Central Sulawesi is one of Indonesia’s least visited provinces, and the Banggai archipelago is its quietest corner. The region sits off the eastern coast of Sulawesi, scattered across the sea between Sulawesi and the Maluku Islands. It remains largely unknown to international travellers, which is precisely why it appeals to those who have already done the well-trodden routes.

The draw is a combination of landscapes that do not fit neatly into a single category. There are lakes so clear that boats appear to float on air. There are waterfalls reached by jungle hikes and beaches where the only footprints are your own. There are reefs that see very few divers, and traditional villages where life still follows the rhythm of tide and wind. The infrastructure is basic. The comfort level is not resort-grade. But if you are willing to trade convenience for something harder to find, Banggai delivers.

For travellers based in Australia, there is a practical appeal too. New flight connections are gradually opening up the region. A direct route between Darwin and Lombok was announced for 2026, and plans for Perth-Lombok services are in discussion. More importantly for Central Sulawesi, a Jakarta-Luwuk route was inaugurated in 2026, with service beginning on 1 June. Domestic connections through Makassar have also expanded, with Indonesia AirAsia linking Luwuk to Surabaya, Kendari, and Palu via Makassar starting in March 2026. These changes do not make Banggai easy to reach, but they make it possible.

## Getting There: The Luwuk Gateway

Almost every journey to the Banggai Islands begins in Luwuk, a small port town on Sulawesi’s eastern peninsula. Luwuk is the administrative and transport hub for the region, and it is where you will arrive by air before continuing by sea.

### Flights to Luwuk (LUW)

Luwuk is served by Syukuran Aminuddin Amir Airport (LUW). Domestic connections are available from Makassar, Jakarta, and Bali, though most routes require a transit in Makassar. From Makassar, daily flights take roughly one to two hours. If you are coming from Australia, the usual routing is a flight to Bali or Jakarta, then a connection through Makassar to Luwuk. The new Jakarta-Luwuk route, opened in June 2026, may simplify this for some travellers.

It is worth checking current schedules with your airline or a local travel agent before booking, as routes and frequencies in this part of Indonesia can change with demand and season.

### The Two Ports in Luwuk

This is where many first-time visitors make a mistake. Luwuk has two departure points for boats to the islands, and they are not interchangeable. Getting into the wrong taxi can cost you a day.

**Pelabuhan Rakyat** (the people’s port) is where the wooden passenger boats leave for Leme Leme on Peleng Island. It sits close to the centre of town. This is the port most travellers want, especially if Paisupok Lake is your goal. Tickets are bought at the port on the day, from a man with a book. There is no website, no app, and no advance sales.

**The ASDP ferry terminal** is where the roll-on, roll-off ferries depart. These are larger vessels that carry vehicles as well as passengers, and they are the way to go if you are bringing a motorbike or heading to Banggai Laut. Tell your driver which port you want by name. They are not in the same place.

### Ferries and Boats to the Islands

The daily wooden passenger ferry from Pelabuhan Rakyat to Leme Leme on Peleng takes about two hours and departs in the early afternoon, typically around 2 PM. Fares are roughly IDR 55,000. The boat is basic: wooden benches, a few private cabins available for an extra fee, and a journey that is generally calm. Departure times move with the weather and the tide, so treat any schedule as a shape rather than a guarantee.

For a faster crossing, private speedboats can be chartered from Luwuk to Peleng in about one hour. The rate is negotiable and considerably higher than the public ferry, but it gives you flexibility and saves time if you are on a tight itinerary.

If you are heading further out to Banggai Laut, the ferry takes about eight hours and departs in the early afternoon, arriving in practice as an overnight journey. Fares start around IDR 130,000. This route is for travellers with time and a sense of adventure, not for those on a schedule.

## Paisupok Lake: The Reason Many Travellers Come

Paisupok Lake, also written Paisu Pok, is the image that has quietly drawn a small number of travellers to this archipelago. It sits on Peleng Island, in the Luk Panenteng village area of North Bulagi District. The name comes from the local language: *paisu* means water, and *pok* means black. The name is misleading. The water is not black. It is a brilliant, transparent blue-green, so clear that boats appear to hover above the bottom, and sunken tree trunks are visible metres below the surface.

### Getting to Paisupok from Leme Leme

From the ferry landing at Leme Leme, the lake is about 23 kilometres away. The road is unmaintained and the journey by taxi or scooter can take nearly an hour, depending on conditions. You can rent a scooter from local operators in Leme Leme; one traveller reported pre-booking via WhatsApp for around IDR 120,000 per day. A private car or taxi is also an option, and your guesthouse can usually help arrange one.

The final approach involves a short walk of a few minutes from the parking area to the lake itself. There is a small entrance fee, which was reported at IDR 5,000 for entry, plus separate charges for scooter or car parking as of early 2025. These prices are likely to change as the site grows in popularity.

### What to Do at Paisupok

The lake is small, around 1.2 hectares, with depths ranging from about 5 to 15 metres depending on the source. You can swim, snorkel, or rent a small boat or canoe to explore the surface. The clarity of the water makes even simple snorkelling rewarding, as you can see every stone and submerged log beneath you. Local guides are often on hand to row visitors across in traditional boats.

Right beside Paisupok is Paisubatango Lagoon, a body of crystal-clear brackish water that adds another layer to the visit. Together, the lake and lagoon make a natural half-day or full-day excursion.

The best time to visit is early morning, before local tourists arrive and while the light is still soft. Conditions can change with rainfall and season, so it is worth asking locally about water clarity before you make the trip.

## Beyond Paisupok: Beaches, Waterfalls, and Island Life

The Banggai Islands are not a single destination. They are an archipelago of more than a hundred islands, and Peleng is only the largest and most accessible. If you have time beyond Paisupok, there is more to see.

### Poganda Beach

Poganda Beach, on the northern coast of Peleng near Luk Panenteng, is one of the most accessible and rewarding beaches in the region. It offers white sand, calm water, and good snorkelling directly from the shore. There are small shops, paid toilet facilities, and photogenic swings set up along the beach. The entrance fee is minimal, reported at around IDR 2,000 per person, with a separate charge for vehicles. It is a good place to spend a relaxed afternoon after a morning at Paisupok.

### Waterfalls Around Luwuk

Before or after your island trip, the Luwuk mainland has several waterfalls worth a detour. Salodik Waterfall is a multi-tiered cascade with natural pools and jade-green water, often compared to Kuang Si in Laos. Kamumu Waterfall requires a jungle hike and is reached by a drive to Balantak, roughly two to three hours from Luwuk. Chalice Waterfall is another option, known for its turquoise cascades. These are day trips rather than multi-day excursions, and they pair well with a night or two in Luwuk before or after the ferry crossing.

### Diving and Snorkelling

The reefs around the Banggai archipelago are among the least visited in Indonesia. Dive operators describe steep walls, deep channels, and long underwater ledges, with coral starting right at the water’s edge in some places. Snorkellers can find vibrant coral gardens in shallow water, and divers can explore sites that see very few visitors. Because the region is remote, it is best to arrange diving through an operator with proper equipment and a certified dive master. If you are an experienced independent diver, you can ask locally in Leme Leme or Luwuk about current conditions and available guides.

## Practical Considerations for Australian Travellers

### Visas and Entry

Visa rules for Australians entering Indonesia have been the subject of much discussion in recent years. As of the most recent information available, Australians are not part of Indonesia’s visa-free scheme, and visa-on-arrival is the usual route for tourist visits. The situation can change, so check the official Indonesian immigration website or your airline before departure. Do not rely on older blog posts or forum comments.

### Flights and Connections

Most travellers from Australia will connect through Bali or Jakarta to reach Makassar, and then take a domestic flight to Luwuk. Build buffer time into your plans, especially if you have a ferry or a tour booked. Domestic flights in Indonesia can be delayed, and thinner routes may have limited daily options. The new Jakarta-Luwuk route and expanded AirAsia connections via Makassar are improving access, but schedules should still be confirmed close to your travel dates.

### Money and Facilities

Luwuk has ATMs and banks, as well as hotels ranging from budget homestays to mid-range options. Once you leave Luwuk for the islands, facilities become much more basic. Bring enough cash for your time on Peleng, as ATMs are unreliable or nonexistent outside the main towns. There are small shops and simple eateries on the islands, but you should not expect a wide range of services.

### Accommodation

In Luwuk, budget homestays start from around IDR 100,000, with mid-range hotels like City Hotel and higher-end options like Swiss-Belinn and Hotel Santika available. On Peleng, accommodation is limited and basic. Homestays and guesthouses are the norm, often arranged through local contacts or travel agents. Booking in advance is wise, especially if you are travelling between July and September.

### Local Support

If you prefer some logistical support, Banggai Wonderland is a local travel resource that arranges private trips and can help with ferry timings, accommodation, and guides in the region. You can also organise the journey yourself with patience and a willingness to ask around in Luwuk. Both approaches work. The difference is how much time you want to spend on logistics versus how much you want to spend on the water.

### Weather and Conditions

The dry season, roughly April to September, generally offers calmer seas and better visibility. The wet season from November to March can bring rough crossings and reduced boat schedules. Conditions on ferries, roads, and domestic flights can change with weather and local circumstances, so always check current information with your accommodation or a local operator before you rely on a schedule. What worked last month may not work today.

## Is Banggai Right for You?

The Banggai Islands are not for every traveller. They require patience, a tolerance for basic infrastructure, and a willingness to accept that things may not run on time. If you need resorts, reliable Wi-Fi, and a wide choice of restaurants, this is not your destination.

But if you have already done Lombok and you are looking for the next step, the one that takes you further from the crowds and closer to a version of Indonesia that fewer people see, Banggai rewards the effort. Paisupok Lake alone justifies the journey. The reefs, the beaches, and the quiet villages make it worth staying longer. And the fact that you will likely be the only foreign traveller you see for days is not a bug. It is the point.
