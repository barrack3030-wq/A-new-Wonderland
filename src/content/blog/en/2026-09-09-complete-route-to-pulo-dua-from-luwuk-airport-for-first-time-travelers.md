Getting to Know Pulo Dua, a Hidden Paradise in Eastern Sulawesi
Pernahkah Anda membayangkan berdiri di puncak bukit hijau, memandang hamparan laut biru keemasan saat matahari terbenam, dengan deburan ombak yang menenangkan? Itulah Pulo Dua.

Located in North Balantak District, Banggai Regency, Central Sulawesi, Pulo Dua is a marine tourism destination nicknamed the "Padar Island of Sulawesi" because of its visual similarity to Padar Island in Labuan Bajo. The name "Pulo Dua" itself refers to two small peninsulas extending into the sea, creating an iconic and highly photogenic view.

Pulo Dua's uniqueness isn't just from atop the hills. Beneath its ocean surface lies a pristine coral reef with around 35 dive spots of varying difficulty levels, making it a paradise for snorkeling and diving enthusiasts.

However, Pulo Dua's charm also holds historical value. In this area lies Lukapan Hill, former defenses of Japanese soldiers that still retain observation holes, cooking stoves, and bathtubs left behind from World War II.

For beginner travelers, the journey to Pulo Dua may feel challenging. However, with this complete guide, you will be ready to explore one of Indonesia's hidden gems.

Stage 1: Arrival at Syukuran Aminuddin Amir Airport, Luwuk
Syukuran Aminuddin Amir Airport (LUW)
Your journey begins at Syukuran Aminuddin Amir Airport in Luwuk, the capital of Banggai Regency. This airport is located about 11 km from downtown Luwuk and serves domestic flights from various major cities in Indonesia.

Popular flight routes:

From Jakarta: Direct flights to Luwuk are available, with a duration of around 2-3 hours.

From Palu: An alternative if direct flights are unavailable; you can fly to Palu and then continue overland to Luwuk.

Tips for beginners: Book flight tickets well in advance, especially if traveling during holiday seasons or weekends. The best time to visit Pulo Dua is from April to October when the weather is clear and the waters are calm.

Stage 2: From the Airport to Downtown Luwuk
After landing at Luwuk Airport, the first step is heading to the city center to arrange transportation to Pulo Dua.

Transportation Options from Airport to City
Option | Estimated Time | Estimated Cost | Description
Online Ride-Hailing | 20-30 minutes | IDR 20,000 - IDR 40,000 | Most practical for beginners
Airport Taxi | 20-30 minutes | IDR 50,000 - IDR 80,000 | Comfortable, available at the airport
Rental Car | By agreement | IDR 300,000 - IDR 500,000/day | Recommended for onward travel

Tips: If you plan to rent a vehicle for the journey to Pulo Dua, it is more economical to rent directly from the airport or arrange a pickup with a travel provider beforehand.

Stage 3: Overland Journey from Luwuk to Kampangar Village
This is the longest stage of your journey. From Luwuk City, you must travel overland to Kampangar Village (or Pulo Dua Village), North Balantak District.

Overland Journey Details
Information | Details
Distance | ± 80 - 185 km (depending on route and source)
Travel Time | 2 - 3 hours
Vehicle | Four-wheeled or two-wheeled
Road Conditions | Winding, along the coast with beautiful views

Overland Transportation Options
1. Private Vehicle Rental (Recommended for Beginners)

Since there is no direct public transportation to Pulo Dua, renting a vehicle is the best option.

Rental car: IDR 400,000 - IDR 700,000/day (driver included)

Rental motorbike: IDR 100,000 - IDR 150,000/day

Rental with driver: More comfortable, drivers usually know the route well

2. Travel Agent or Tour Package

Several service providers offer travel packages from Luwuk to Pulo Dua, including land and sea transportation.

3. Intercity Bus

Boyou Terminal in Luwuk serves inter-district buses heading towards Balantak. However, this is less recommended for beginners as it requires additional vehicle transfers.

Scenery Along the Way
This land trip is not just a transfer—you will be treated to breathtaking coastal panoramas of Central Sulawesi. An expanse of sea on one side and green rolling hills on the other will accompany your journey.

Important tips for beginners:

Bring snacks/supplies — long journey with minimal rest areas

Bring a power bank — internet signal may be limited in certain spots

Get enough rest — do not push yourself if exhausted

Note emergency numbers — make sure your phone has credit and data

Stage 4: Crossing from Kampangar Village to Pulo Dua
Upon arriving in Kampangar Village (or Pulo Dua Village), your journey is not over yet. Pulo Dua is an island separated from the main landmass, so you must cross using a boat.

Boat Crossing Details
Information | Details
Travel Time | 10 - 15 minutes
Boat Type | Fisherman's boat / speedboat
Capacity | Up to 8 people per boat
Rental Fee | IDR 350,000 - IDR 400,000 per boat (one way)

Things to Consider When Crossing
Negotiate prices before boarding the boat

Wear a life jacket — especially if sea conditions are choppy

Bring waterproof gear/bags for your bags and electronics

Pay attention to the weather — crossings can be canceled if waves are too large

If you are lucky, you might even spot dolphins swimming around the boat during the trip.

Stage 5: Arriving at Pulo Dua — Paradise Awaits
Congratulations! You have arrived at Pulo Dua. Now it is time to enjoy everything this exotic destination has to offer.

Activities to Do
✅ Hiking Pulo Dua Hill
From the hilltop, you can enjoy panoramas of the blue sea and two iconic peninsulas that characterize Pulo Dua. Colorful paved steps/trails are available to facilitate the climb.

✅ Snorkeling & Diving
Pulo Dua's underwater beauty is equally mesmerizing. Pristine coral reefs and diverse marine life are ready to be explored.

✅ Relaxing on the White Sand Beach
An expanse of white sand with clear, calm sea waters—the perfect spot to relax.

✅ Camping & Enjoying the Sunset
Staying outdoors under a star-filled sky will be an unforgettable experience.

✅ Exploring Historical Sites
Visit Lukapan Hill to see World War II relics.

Accommodation in Pulo Dua
Although relatively remote, Pulo Dua offers several accommodation options:

Tompotika Resort (Tompotika Dive Lodge) | Varies | A more representative/upscale option
Local Homestay | IDR 100,000 - IDR 200,000/night | Experience staying with local residents

Note: The number of accommodations in Pulo Dua is still limited. It is highly recommended to book well in advance, especially when traveling during peak season.

Return Route (Pulo Dua to Luwuk Airport)
The return trip follows the same route in reverse:

Pulo Dua → Kampangar Village (10-15 min boat, cost IDR 350,000 - IDR 400,000)

Kampangar Village → Luwuk (2-3 hrs land, rental vehicle)

Luwuk → Syukuran Aminuddin Amir Airport (20-30 min)

Important tip: Ensure you already have flight tickets and leave a buffer time of at least 4-5 hours from your departure from Pulo Dua to your scheduled flight. Land and sea travel can take longer than estimated due to weather or road conditions.

Important Tips for Beginner Travelers
Before Departure
Research weather — visit between April-October

Book flight tickets & accommodations well in advance

Prepare physically — long land journey and hiking terrain

Bring gear — sunscreen, hat, swimming goggles, life jacket, power bank, personal medicines

Download offline maps — internet signal is limited

During the Trip
Respect local culture — the ethnic Balantak people have pristine traditions

Keep clean — bring personal trash bags

Do not remove marine life — protect pristine ecosystems

Follow management guidelines — especially regarding safety while snorkeling/diving

Security / Safety
Note emergency numbers — Police, Ambulance, and lodging contacts

Share your itinerary with family or friends

Bring enough cash — ATMs are limited in Luwuk and nonexistent in Pulo Dua

Conclusion: Is Pulo Dua Worth Visiting?
Pulo Dua is a destination highly worth visiting—especially for those seeking tranquility, pristine natural beauty, and a unique experience away from mainstream tourist spots.

Yes, the journey is long and requires extra preparation. But every drop of sweat along the way will be fully rewarded when you stand on the peak of Pulo Dua hill, watching the sunset between two peninsulas, with the blue sea stretching as far as the eye can see.

As many travelers say: "Pulo Dua is not just a destination; it is an experience that stays in your heart."
