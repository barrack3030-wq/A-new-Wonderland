Bali has long been Indonesia’s main gateway for international travelers. However, if you have already visited Bali or want to explore a quieter, clearer, and more authentic side of Indonesia, there is one region worth considering: Banggai Regency and Banggai Kepulauan in Central Sulawesi. Here, you will find glass-clear lakes, secluded white-sand beaches, pristine coral reefs, and endemic fish found only in these waters.

This article will introduce you to 10 destinations around Luwuk and the Banggai Islands. These ten places can be your reason to fly all the way to Central Sulawesi, far from the crowds of Bali. They are all still within Indonesia, and they are all located beyond Bali.

## How to Reach Luwuk and Banggai

Luwuk is the capital of Banggai Regency and the main gateway to the Banggai Islands. Syukuran Aminuddin Amir Airport (LUW) serves flights from Jakarta and Makassar. If you are coming from Bali or another city, you will usually need to transit through one of these hubs. Flight routes may change, so check the latest schedules before booking your ticket.

From Luwuk, the islands of the Banggai Islands can only be reached by sea. Ferries and wooden boats operate from Luwuk People’s Port to Peling Island (Leme Leme) and Salakan. Schedules, prices, and availability depend heavily on the weather and wave conditions. Always confirm the latest schedule with the port or a local agent before departing. For certain destinations such as Lake Paisu Pok, speedboats from Lalong Bay usually depart in the morning.

## 10 Destinations in Banggai and Surrounding Areas

### 1. Lake Paisu Pok

Lake Paisu Pok in Luk Panenteng Village, North Bulagi District, is an icon of Banggai Kepulauan. Its water is exceptionally clear—you can see the lakebed along with submerged tree trunks. The water ranges in color from blue to green, and its surface is often as still as glass. The best time to visit the lake is from morning until late morning, when the light is still soft. Entrance fees and boat or gazebo rentals apply, so bring cash.

### 2. Poganda Beach

Also located in Luk Panenteng Village, Poganda Beach offers fine white sand and calm seawater. The beach is ideal for swimming, snorkeling, and diving. From the small pier, you can dive straight in and enjoy the well-preserved coral reefs. Because it is close to Lake Paisu Pok, many travelers visit both in a single day.

### 3. Pulo Dua Balantak

Pulo Dua is located in North Balantak District, Banggai Regency. To reach it, you can travel overland from Luwuk to Kampangar Village, then continue by boat. The island offers a combination of green hills, white-sand beaches, and clear waters. Activities include snorkeling, diving, swimming, and hiking up the hills to enjoy sunrise or sunset views.

### 4. Kilo 5 Beach

For those who want to enjoy the beach without crossing to another island, Kilo 5 Beach in Maahas Subdistrict, South Luwuk District, is an excellent choice. It is located close to central Luwuk. The beach offers white sand and clear seawater, with coral reefs visible beneath the surface.

### 5. Piala Waterfall

Piala Waterfall is one of the most popular waterfalls in Central Sulawesi. Located not far from Luwuk, this two-tiered waterfall has exceptionally clear water and is surrounded by lush trees. You will need to trek a little to reach it, but the scenery and fresh air around it are well worth the effort.

### 6. Peling Island (Leme Leme)

Peling Island, with its small town of Leme Leme, is one of the main islands in the Banggai Islands. The island can be reached by ferry from Luwuk. Here, you can find fishing villages, secluded beaches, and an island atmosphere that remains highly natural. Peling Island is also a gateway to various snorkeling and diving spots in the surrounding area.

### 7. Salakan

Salakan is the capital of Banggai Kepulauan Regency. This small town serves as an administrative and trading center for the islands. Although it is not a primary tourist destination, Salakan is important as a transit point and a place to find accommodation, food, and basic necessities before exploring other islands. The town’s simple atmosphere and local market can be an experience in themselves.

### 8. Tongo Island

Tongo Island is one of the snorkeling and diving spots known in the Banggai Islands. Its waters are home to the Banggai Cardinalfish, a small endemic fish found only in this region. The fish usually inhabit depths of 0 to 16 meters and often shelter among sea urchin spines. Underwater visibility can be excellent during the dry season.

### 9. Bandang Island

Bandang Island is also an important diving location in the Banggai Islands. Together with Tongo Island, Bone-bone, Tolobundo, Bulutan, and Tropot, it is a place to observe Banggai Cardinalfish in their natural habitat. If you are a diver or snorkeler, these places deserve a spot on your list.

### 10. Banggai Cardinalfish Diving Spots (Bone-bone, Tolobundo, Bulutan, Tropot)

This group of diving spots is scattered throughout the waters of the Banggai Islands. Each has its own character, but all offer the opportunity to see Banggai Cardinalfish. This endemic fish lives only in Banggai waters, making it a compelling reason to dive here. Bring your own snorkeling or diving equipment, as fully equipped rentals are not always available.

## Best Time to Visit

The dry season, from around April to November, is the best time to visit Luwuk and the Banggai Islands. During this period, the sea is relatively calm, the weather is clear, and underwater visibility is at its best. The peak of the dry season usually occurs between May and September.

By contrast, December to March is the rainy season, with higher rainfall and seas that can be rougher. If you still wish to visit during this period, prepare a backup plan because boat schedules may change. Keep in mind that July, August, and the period around Eid al-Fitr are busy. If you travel during these times, it is best to book accommodation and transportation well in advance.

## Practical Tips Before You Go

 - Bring enough cash. ATMs may be available in Luwuk and Salakan, but not on the smaller islands.
 - Prepare your own snorkeling equipment. Although rentals are available in some locations, their quality and availability are not always guaranteed.
 - Use sunscreen and sun protection. Hilly and beach areas are often open, without shade.
 - Learn a few Indonesian phrases. English proficiency is limited in Luwuk and the surrounding islands.
 - Check the latest conditions before departing. Weather, boat schedules, and road access can change. For a more organized trip, you may consider a local operator such as Banggai Wonderland, which offers private-trip packages and up-to-date information about conditions on the ground.

## Conclusion

Banggai Island, Luwuk, and the surrounding areas are destinations suited to those seeking a quieter and more authentic nature adventure after Bali. With careful planning—especially regarding sea transportation and the timing of your visit—you can enjoy glass-clear lakes, secluded beaches, and underwater biodiversity rarely found elsewhere. This is not an instant, effortless journey, but that is precisely where its appeal lies.
