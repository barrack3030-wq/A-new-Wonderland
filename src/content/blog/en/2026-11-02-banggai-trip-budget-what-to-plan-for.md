"How much does a Banggai trip cost?" is one of the most common questions we hear, and the honest answer is that it depends on four choices: how you cross the sea, where you sleep, whether you travel with a guide and how many people share the costs. This article does not give a single number. It shows how to break the budget into parts, so you can compare quotes and avoid surprises.

All figures below are **indicative**. They come from public travel reports and from other pages on this site, they were not collected from a fresh survey, and prices in the islands change. Confirm every amount before you commit.

## 1. Getting from Luwuk to the islands

This is usually the biggest decision.

- **Public ferry to Leme-Leme (Peleng).** A traveler report quoted in our [Paisu Pok guide](/en/blog/2026-09-14-what-to-know-before-visiting-paisu-pok-lake/) gives about IDR 54,000 per person, with a daily departure around 2:00 PM and a crossing of roughly 2 hours. A small private cabin for two was reported at about IDR 50,000 extra. If the return fare is the same, a round trip would be around IDR 108,000 per person, though you should check that.
- **Private speedboat.** The same report gives IDR 1.5 to 4 million round trip for a crossing of about 1 hour, depending on boat size and route. The boat is chartered, not sold per seat, so the cost per person falls as your group grows. For example, IDR 1.5 million shared by 4 people is about IDR 375,000 each; IDR 4 million shared by 4 is IDR 1 million each.
- **Longer crossings.** For Mbuang-Mbuang, our [September guide](/en/blog/2026-09-10-is-september-the-right-time-to-visit-mbuang-mbuang/) mentions a regular boat of about 7 hours at roughly IDR 55,000, or a speedboat of about 2 hours at around IDR 2 million round trip for 5 to 8 people. That is roughly IDR 250,000 to 400,000 per person if the boat is full.

The trade-off is time versus money. A public ferry costs far less but runs on a fixed schedule; a speedboat lets you set the timing but costs more unless shared. For route details, see [how to get to the Banggai Islands from Luwuk](/en/blog/2026-09-18-how-to-get-to-banggai-islands-from-luwuk/).

## 2. Where you sleep

On Peleng and the smaller islands, homestays are the main option. Our Paisu Pok guide mentions homestays in Luk Panenteng at around IDR 150,000 to 250,000 per night. Rooms are simple, so ask whether there is a fan, a private bathroom and a meal included. In Luwuk itself, hotels cover a wider range, so check current rates directly.

## 3. Local transport on the island

Roads can be difficult when wet. A route guide on this site gives rental motorbikes at about IDR 100,000 to 150,000 per day, and a report from a 2025 travel blog mentions around IDR 150,000. A motorbike is cheap, but ask about road conditions before you ride, especially after rain. A driver or a car arranged through your accommodation will cost more but can be safer for luggage and for travelers who are not confident on two wheels.

## 4. Guide or tour operator

Some travelers go independently; others book a guide or a package. A package should state what it covers. Our own package pages list private transport, a local guide, accommodation and entrance fees for included destinations, and exclude flights, snorkeling equipment, personal expenses and travel insurance. We do not publish fixed prices on those pages, so ask for a written quote.

When you compare two quotes, line up the same items: boat, accommodation, meals, entrance fees, guide, transfers and extras. A lower headline price often leaves out something the higher one includes. Our guide to [choosing a Banggai tour package](/en/blog/2026-10-26-how-to-choose-banggai-tour-package/) covers this in more depth.

## 5. Food, tickets and small costs

Food options in smaller villages can be simple and limited, so ask your homestay what meals they can prepare and at what price. Entrance or parking fees may apply at some sites. Water, snacks, insect repellent and a rain cover for your bag add up. Snorkeling gear may need to be rented or brought; operators often list it as an extra.

## 6. Cash and a buffer

Plan to carry **cash**. A 2025 travel report notes that cash is often the only option in the islands, and card machines or ATMs may not be available outside Luwuk and larger towns. Withdraw enough in Luwuk before you leave. Add a buffer of roughly 15 to 20 percent for weather delays, an extra night or a change of plan. That percentage is a simple rule of thumb, not a published figure.

## A simple budgeting worksheet

| Item | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Sea crossing | Public ferry or chartered boat? How many people share? |
| Accommodation | How many nights, and which village? |
| Local transport | Motorbike, car or driver? |
| Guide or package | What exactly is included? |
| Food and fees | Which meals, tickets and gear are extra? |
| Cash and buffer | How much cash before leaving Luwuk? |
