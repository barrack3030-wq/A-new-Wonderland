# Paisu Batango: The Spring Where Three Waters Meet

Paisu Batango is a **coastal freshwater spring and lagoon** in **Desa Luk Panenteng, Kecamatan Bulagi Utara, Kabupaten Banggai Kepulauan, Central Sulawesi**, on **Peleng Island**. It sits on the southern side of the village, about **15 minutes before** the better-known **Paisu Pok Lake**. Many travelers pass it on the way to Paisu Pok without realizing that it is a destination in its own right.

The name explains the setting. In the local language, **paisu means water** and **batango means tree trunk**, referring to spring water emerging near the base of a tree trunk and forming a small pool. It is not a conventional lake, but a **tidal spring pool** where different types of water meet.

## The Three Waters and the Tidal Pool

Paisu Batango is unusual because it can bring together **freshwater, brackish water and seawater**. At **low tide**, the spring water dominates and the pool feels clear and fresh. At **high tide**, seawater enters and the water becomes more brackish or salty.

Local accounts also describe **two water temperatures**, with warm and cool water where spring flow and seawater currents meet. The pool is shallow and clear, surrounded by **rocks, tree roots and dense greenery**. Because the balance changes with the tide, the character of the pool can be different from one visit to another.

## The Site: Dock, Gazebos and Village Use

Paisu Batango has a small set of facilities for a remote village attraction. The provincial tourism office lists a **small dock, footpaths, eight gazebos, a guard room and a non-permanent mushala**. The gazebos provide shade and make the site suitable for a short rest or picnic.

It is also a **working village water source**. Residents use the area for bathing and washing and collect water from rock holes for household needs. Visitors should therefore treat the pool as a shared community space, respect local activity and avoid anything that could pollute the water.

Access is generally **free or based on a small local contribution**, although tourism fees around the Paisu area may change as management develops.

## Swimming, Snorkelling and Photography

**Swimming** is possible, although the depth changes with the tide. The pool is shallow compared with a conventional lake. **Snorkelling** is not the main attraction because the site is small and the bottom is mostly sand and rock, but the clear water makes it pleasant for wading and floating.

The surrounding rocks, tree roots and blue-green water also make Paisu Batango a good place for **photography**. The gazebos make it easy to turn the stop into a simple picnic break before or after visiting Paisu Pok.

## How to Get to Paisu Batango

There are two main routes.

**By road from Salakan:** From **Salakan**, the regency capital, the journey to Desa Luk Panenteng takes about **3.5 hours** by car or motorcycle. The route passes through green hills and coastal scenery. Paisu Batango is on the southern side of the village, before Paisu Pok.

**By speedboat from Luwuk:** From **Luwuk**, speedboats can take around **1 hour** to the Luk Panenteng area. A boat for about 10 people has been reported at around **Rp4 million**, subject to local arrangements and current prices. This route can be combined with **Paisu Batango, Paisu Pok and Poganda Beach** in one day.

Another option is the public ferry from Luwuk to **Salakan**, followed by an overnight stay and a road journey to Luk Panenteng the next day.

## Best Time to Visit

The **dry season, roughly May to October**, generally offers more stable road and sea conditions. For the freshest water, visit around **low tide**. At high tide, seawater enters the pool and the character becomes more brackish.

**Morning** is usually cooler and quieter, especially for travelers combining Paisu Batango with Paisu Pok.

## What to Bring and What to Expect

**Bring:** Cash, drinking water, insect repellent, a change of clothes if you plan to swim, and a camera.

**Do not expect:** A formal ticket office, changing rooms, showers, restaurants or a managed swimming area. Paisu Batango is a **village spring with basic facilities**, not a resort.

## FAQ

### Is Paisu Batango the same as Paisu Pok?

No. They are **two separate sites in the same village**. Paisu Pok is the larger freshwater lake on the northern side of Luk Panenteng, while Paisu Batango is the smaller coastal spring on the southern side.

### Can you swim at Paisu Batango?

Yes. Swimming is possible, but the depth changes with the tide. The pool is also used by local residents, so visitors should be respectful and keep the water clean.

### Is there an entrance fee?

It is generally **free or based on a small local contribution**. Fees may change as local tourism management develops.

### How do you get to Paisu Batango from Luwuk?

The faster option is a **speedboat**, taking about one hour depending on the departure point and sea conditions. Another route is the ferry to Salakan followed by a road journey of about 3.5 hours to Luk Panenteng.

### When is the best time to see the freshwater character?

Around **low tide**, when spring water dominates the pool. At high tide, seawater enters and the water becomes more brackish.

## Related Destinations

**Paisu Pok Lake** is the better-known freshwater lake in Luk Panenteng, about 15 minutes farther into the village. **Poganda Beach** is around 2 km south and offers white sand and snorkelling. On Banggai Island, **Bontolan Beach** and **Oyama Beach** are major coastal stops. Travelers based in Luwuk can also visit **Kilo 5 Beach** and **Piala Waterfall**.
