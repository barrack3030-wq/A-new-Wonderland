# Oyama Beach Banggai: Boat Access, Snorkeling and Travel Guide

Oyama Beach is a white-sand beach on the **northern tip of Banggai Island in Banggai Laut Regency, Central Sulawesi**. This guide covers how to get to Oyama Beach, boat access from Lokotoy or Popisi, snorkeling, the best time to visit, and what to bring. It sits in the **Lokotoy / Pasir Putih area of North Banggai District**, but the exact village name varies between sources—some place it in Desa Lokotoy, others in Desa Pasir Putih. What is consistent is the setting: fine white sand, calm turquoise water, and a large mangrove forest directly behind the shoreline.

The beach is **not accessible by road**. The northern tip of Banggai Island is cut off by the mangrove, so the only way in is by boat—typically a short crossing from the villages of **Lokotoy** or **Popisi**. This is the main practical constraint, and also the reason Oyama remains quieter than beaches like Bontolan or Kilo 5.

## The Beach and the Mangrove

Oyama is a long, open beach rather than a small cove. The sand is **soft and white**, the water shifts from pale turquoise in the shallows to deeper blue further out, and the waves are **gentle enough to be safe for children**. Green islands sit offshore, giving the view a layered, sheltered feel.

Behind the beach is a **mangrove forest**. It is not just a backdrop—it is the reason the northern tip is inaccessible by road, and it supports wildlife. One travel account mentions **iguanas and green lizards** in the mangrove area behind the white-sand beach. A walkway into the mangrove existed in the past, though reports from 2021 noted it was falling apart; conditions may have changed since.

The beach has **no entrance fee and no official opening hours**. Facilities are basic but present: **parking, public toilets, a mushala, selfie spots, and food stalls**. There is **no homestay** on site according to the latest tourism village data, so overnight stays require returning to Lokotoy or Banggai town.

## Swimming, Snorkelling, and Dive Spots

Swimming is the main activity. The water is calm and clear, and the shallow shelf makes it comfortable for wading and beginner swimming. **Snorkelling** is possible from the beach, and the underwater scenery is described as having healthy coral and tropical fish.

Two named **dive spots** are associated with the Oyama area: **Batu Alay** and **Batu Karang**. These are reef sites rather than deep dives, and local boat operators can take snorkellers and divers to them. Equipment is not available for rent at the beach itself, so bring your own mask and fins.

**Speedboat rental** is available in the area, with reported rates starting around **Rp1–1.5 million** for a boat. For simply reaching Oyama from Lokotoy or Popisi, the crossing is short—roughly 3 km—and in 2020–2021 cost approximately **Rp150,000 per boat from Lokotoy** or **Rp50,000 per boat from Popisi**. Prices may have changed, and travellers should confirm locally.

## How to Get to Oyama Beach: Lokotoy or Popisi

There is no road to Oyama. The standard approach is:

**Step 1: Reach Banggai Laut.** From Luwuk, take a ferry or boat to **Banggai town**, the regency capital. The crossing takes several hours depending on the vessel. Some travellers arrive via **Jeti Tobing in Kalumbatan** (Banggai Kepulauan), with a short speedboat crossing to the Banggai Laut side, then a 10–15 minute boat ride to Oyama.

**Step 2: Go to Lokotoy or Popisi.** From Banggai town, travel by road to **Lokotoy** (approximately 1.5 hours) or **Popisi**, a Bajo village closer to the beach.

**Step 3: Boat to Oyama.** Arrange a local boat. From Lokotoy, the crossing is short but pricing has historically been higher (around Rp150,000 per boat). From Popisi, the crossing was cheaper (around Rp50,000 per boat) and some travellers report more willing boatmen.

## What to Bring and What to Expect

**Bring:** Drinking water, snacks, sun protection, a mat or sarong if you plan to sit on the sand, snorkelling gear, and cash for the boat and any food stalls.

**Do not expect:** Road access, accommodation on the beach, equipment rental, reliable mobile signal, or formal management. Oyama is a **bring-everything beach**.

## Best Time to Visit

The **dry season (roughly May to October)** offers the calmest seas and clearest water. The beach is best visited in the **morning or late afternoon**; midday sun is strong, though sea breeze makes it feel less intense than it is. **Sunset** is a highlight—travellers consistently describe the sky turning soft orange over the water, with reflections on the calm surface.

## FAQ

### Is Oyama Beach the same as Bontolan Beach?

No. They are **separate beaches** on Banggai Island. Bontolan is in Bone Baru Village and is road-accessible; Oyama is on the northern tip and is **boat-only**. They are often visited on the same boat day, with Oyama reached from Lokotoy or Popisi and Bontolan from Bone Baru.

### Can you reach Oyama Beach by road?

No. The northern tip is blocked by mangrove, so **boat access is the only option**. The nearest road access is in Lokotoy or Popisi.

### Is there an entrance fee?

No. Oyama Beach is **free to enter**. You pay only for the boat.

### Can you snorkel at Oyama Beach?

Yes. Snorkelling is possible from the beach, and nearby dive spots include **Batu Alay** and **Batu Karang**. Bring your own gear, as rental is not available on site.

### Is there accommodation at Oyama Beach?

No. There is **no homestay on the beach**. The nearest accommodation is in Lokotoy or Banggai town.

## Related Destinations

**Pantai Bontolan** in Bone Baru is often combined with Oyama on the same boat trip. **Long Beach (Pantai Pasir Panjang)** is another boat-access beach on the Banggai Laut side. **Tanjung Gundul** is a nearby limestone cliff with a sea-carved hole. For travellers on Peleng Island, **Paisu Pok Lake** and **Paisu Batango Spring** are the region’s signature freshwater destinations.
