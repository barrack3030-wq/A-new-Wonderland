# Paisu Pok Lake: The Clear Blue Lake of the Banggai Islands

Paisu Pok Lake is a **freshwater lake in Desa Luk Panenteng, Kecamatan Bulagi Utara, Kabupaten Banggai Kepulauan, Central Sulawesi**, on **Peleng Island**. It covers approximately **1.2 hectares**, with a depth commonly reported at **5 to 15 meters**, and sits at the foot of green hills surrounded by coconut palms and dense forest.

The name comes from the local language: **"paisu" means water** and **"pok" means black**—a reference to the dark appearance of the lake bed. The water itself is remarkably transparent, ranging from blue to green-blue depending on the light and time of day.

## The Water and the Lake Bed

Paisu Pok is a **landlocked freshwater lake**, distinct from nearby Paisu Batango, which is a coastal tidal spring. In clear conditions, visibility can reach around **10–15 meters**. Submerged dead trees are visible beneath the surface, creating an unusual underwater landscape.

The water color changes during the day. Early in the morning it can look darker, while later light can bring out vivid blue-green tones and strong reflections. From the lake edge or a canoe, the clarity can make boats appear to float above the lake bed.

## Swimming, Snorkeling, and Boating

**Swimming** is the main activity. The lake is calm and freshwater. **Snorkeling** allows visitors to see submerged trees, rocks and small fish.

**Canoes and paddleboards** are also available, making it easy to explore the lake surface slowly. Photography is another major attraction, especially when the water is calm and reflective.

## Facilities and Rental Prices

Paisu Pok is managed by the **village government through BUMDes Paisu Batango**. A formal cooperation and revenue-sharing arrangement with the Banggai Kepulauan Tourism Office was finalized in September 2026, according to the supplied research. The provincial tourism office also lists Paisu Pok among the attractions of Luk Panenteng and records basic facilities including a dock, footpath, boats, gazebos, a guard room and a non-permanent prayer room. citeturn596339search1turn596339search3

**Typical reported charges:**
- Lake entrance: **Rp5,000–10,000 per person**
- Motorcycle parking: **Rp3,000**
- Car parking: **Rp10,000**
- Canoe: **Rp30,000**
- Paddleboard: **Rp100,000**
- Snorkeling gear: **Rp30,000–50,000 per set**
- Gazebo: **Rp30,000**
- GoPro rental: **Rp200,000**, including photographer according to the supplied source
- Camping: **Rp35,000 per day** with your own equipment

Rates can change, so confirm locally before paying.

## How to Get to Paisu Pok

There are two main routes from Luwuk.

**By public ferry:** Fly to **Luwuk** and continue from Luwuk People's Port to **Leme Leme** on Peleng Island. The supplied research reports a crossing of about **2 hours** and a fare around **Rp54,000 per person**. From Leme Leme, continue roughly **23 km by road**, usually around **1 hour** depending on conditions.

**By speedboat:** A private speedboat can reach the area in around **1 hour**, with reported round-trip costs of approximately **Rp1.5–4 million** depending on vessel and group size.

From the parking area in Luk Panenteng, the lake is reached by a short **5–15 minute walk**.

## Where to Stay

Accommodation is available in **Luk Panenteng** and the surrounding area, with simple homestays and guesthouses. The supplied research lists example rates of around **Rp150,000–250,000 per night** for a basic homestay and **Rp100,000–200,000** for simple village guesthouses.

**Salakan** offers more accommodation options and can serve as an overnight base before or after visiting the lake. Camping beside the lake is also possible for the reported camping fee, provided you bring your own equipment.

## Best Time to Visit

The **dry season, roughly April to November**, is generally the more comfortable period for road and boat travel. For photography, **afternoon** can bring brighter blue-green water than early morning.

The lake is accessible throughout the day, but visitors should plan transport carefully so they are not travelling unfamiliar roads or crossing by boat after dark. Weekdays are usually quieter than weekends and public holidays.

## What to Bring and What to Expect

**Bring:** Cash, drinking water, insect repellent, swimwear, a dry bag, and your own snorkeling gear if preferred.

**Do not expect:** Reliable mobile signal, extensive commercial services, or a large resort-style operation. Paisu Pok remains a **village-run destination** with practical facilities and a slower pace.

## FAQ

### Is Paisu Pok the same as Paisu Batango?

No. They are **separate sites in Luk Panenteng**. Paisu Pok is the larger freshwater lake; Paisu Batango is the smaller coastal spring on the southern side of the village.

### Can you swim in Paisu Pok Lake?

Yes. Swimming is one of the main activities, and the lake is calm and freshwater. Always follow local safety guidance.

### How deep is Paisu Pok Lake?

Most supplied sources place the depth at approximately **5–15 meters**, with some reports giving a deeper range. Visibility is often described around **10–15 meters** in clear conditions.

### Do I need a guide?

Not strictly. The lake is straightforward to reach once you are in Luk Panenteng, but local guides or boat operators can simplify transport and activity arrangements.

### Is there accommodation nearby?

Yes. Simple homestays and guesthouses are available around Luk Panenteng, with additional options in Salakan.

## Related Destinations

**Paisu Batango** is a short distance away and offers a different water environment where freshwater, brackish water and seawater meet. **Poganda Beach** is another nearby coastal attraction. On Banggai Island, **Pantai Bontolan** and **Oyama Beach** are major coastal stops, while **Kilo 5 Beach** and **Piala Waterfall** are practical options for travelers based in Luwuk.
